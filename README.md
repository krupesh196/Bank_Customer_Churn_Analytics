The Bank Customer Churn Analytics project is a data-driven initiative aimed at identifying why customers leave a financial institution. Customer retention is one of the most critical metrics for banks, as acquiring a new customer is significantly more expensive than retaining an existing one.

This project analyzes a comprehensive dataset of bank customers to uncover behavioral patterns and demographic factors correlated with churn. By processing this data into interactive dashboards, this project enables stakeholders to proactively identify high-risk accounts and implement targeted retention campaigns before a customer decides to leave.

Key Features
Customer Segmentation: Analysis of customer demographics, including age, geography, and gender, to see which groups have the highest churn rates.

Account Behavior Analysis: Tracking how account balances, the number of products held (like credit cards or loans), and account tenure influence customer loyalty.

Activity Monitoring: Evaluating the impact of active vs. inactive membership status on the likelihood of a customer leaving the bank.

Risk Profiling: Correlating credit scores and estimated salaries with churn to understand the financial profile of leaving customers.

Tech Stack Used
Excel: Utilized for data exploration, cleaning, and formatting raw data files.

SQL: Used for writing complex queries to extract, filter, and aggregate customer data for analysis.

Power BI: Deployed to create an interactive dashboard that visualizes churn metrics, allowing users to drill down into specific customer segments.

Business Value
The insights generated from this analysis provide actionable intelligence for bank management. By understanding exactly who is leaving and why, the bank can offer personalized incentives (like better interest rates or waived fees) to at-risk customers, ultimately protecting the bank's revenue stream and improving customer satisfaction.
